Three ways to sell

Most systems force you into one box. You do not run one kind of business.

A single catalogue line can be a product you count, a service you perform, or an asset you hire out by the hour, day, week or month. The till handles all three, and the reports tell them apart.

01 / Product

Stock you count and sell

Physical goods with SKUs, categories, sizes or your own units: kilograms, sacks, litres, boxes, whatever your trade measures in.

  • Per-location quantities with low-stock warnings
  • An append-only movement ledger behind every count
  • Opening, sold and closing stock in the catalogue report
02 / Service

Work you bill for

A haircut, a repair, a consultation, a cleaning crew for the afternoon. Sold at the till like anything else, with no stock row to maintain.

  • Sold at the till, no inventory attached
  • Priced per visit, per hour, per project or per month
  • Lands on the same receipt as your products
03 / Rental

Assets you hire out

Power tools, decor, gowns, vehicles. Rate cards by hour, day, week or month; deposits, late fees and a full unit lifecycle tracked to the asset.

  • Every physical unit tracked: available, reserved, on hire
  • Deposits held as liability, settled at return
  • Revenue recognised when the asset comes back, not before

What is inside

The whole business, not just the checkout screen.

Every module runs against the same catalogue, the same customers and the same ledger, so the number on the dashboard is the number in the stock room.

Terminal & dashboard

A till that keeps up with the queue

Search by name or SKU, build the cart, attach the customer, apply the voucher, take cash, card, mobile money, bank transfer or credit, and close in one atomic step. Stock cannot oversell, even when two tills race for the last unit.

Catalogue shaped to your trade

Pick your business type at sign-up and your categories, units and SKU prefix arrive pre-filled. Change any of them; the suggestions never lock you in.

Loyalty that pays its own way

Visits and spend update themselves after every sale. Bronze, silver and gold tiers mint a 10% voucher the moment a customer moves up. No cards to print, no spreadsheets to keep.

Reports you can argue with a supplier about

Sales by day, week or month. Revenue against expenses. Top sellers. Credit owed. A catalogue report that reconstructs opening, stock in, sold and closing from the ledger.

Money in, money out

Log expenses by category and location so the balance sheet reflects the shop, not just the till. Credit sales are tracked and reported separately from cash.

Permissions enforced where the data lives

Cashiers run the till. Managers edit the catalogue and issue vouchers. Owners hold settings, staff and multiple locations. The rules are enforced by the database itself. A restricted screen is not a security decision, and we do not treat it as one.

Security

Enforced in the database, not just the interface.

Hiding a button is not a permission model. Every row of your data carries your business identifier, and the database refuses to answer for anyone else, even if a request is crafted by hand and sent straight to the server.

How the boundaries work
  • Isolation Every business is a tenant. Two businesses never see each other's rows. Missing or forged headers match nothing, and the system fails closed.
  • Roles Cashier, manager and owner, checked server-side on every write. Screens render for your role; the database re-checks it.
  • Checkout One atomic transaction: lock, validate, deduct, record, burn the voucher. Two tills cannot oversell the last unit between them.
  • Sign-in Email and password with breach protection and a twelve-character minimum. Multi-factor authentication is available for owners and managers.
